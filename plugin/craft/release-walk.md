Craft note (release, step "walk" — the script's 6.1 Store & Launch for a store BMUZ never submits to: Steam, the phone app stores).
The creative said "Release it". 1.0 is versioned and tagged; the store itself needs their own account, money and art, so they do it, one step at a time, with you beside them. BMUZ never submits to a store and never marks it live on its own.
Read .bmuz/release/run.json ("current": the platform; its "you" list), .planning/release/<platform>-steps.md (the steps, numbered) and the store page in .planning/release/<platform>-page.md. The research behind them: .planning/research/release-<platform>.md (each requirement with its source).

One step at a time: mcp__bmuz__say {"line": "release.walk", "slots": {"platform": "<the platform's name, as the card said it>", "n": "<the step's number>", "all": "<how many steps>", "step": "<the step, in plain words, from the steps file>"}} is your whole reply; end your turn. Their next message ("done", or a question) is the answer. A question: answer it from the research report (name its source), then say the same step again the same way. "Done": the next step, the same way. Never skip one, never do two at once.
2. After the last step: mcp__bmuz__say {"line": "release.walk.live.box", "slots": {"game": "<the game's name>", "platform": "…"}} and ask the box it returns, exactly. "Not yet": say in one plain line that it stays unmarked until it shows there, then mcp__bmuz__say {"wait": true}; ask the box again when they say it's up.
3. "Yes, it's live": mcp__bmuz__say {"line": "release.walk.link", "slots": {"platform": "…", "game": "…"}}: a free question; their next message carries the link.
4. Hand back the link exactly as they typed it. The engine checks three things: their pick in the box, the link in their own typed answer (never a question's words), and the page answering from outside and naming the game; until all three hold nothing says it's live.
