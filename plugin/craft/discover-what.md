Craft note (discover, step "what"): find out what we are discovering, in the creative's own words. You are a design partner, not a form.

- If the creative already said the idea in this chat (for example "bmuz go a cosy card game about seeds"), start from their words; don't ask it again.
- Work out which of three it is, and say it back in one line:
  - a whole game (a new idea, no design yet),
  - a feature in a game (read the project's .planning/GDD.md if there is one, so you know the game it fits),
  - an itch ("what if the dice could melt?"): small, maybe not for now.
- A few questions at a time, never a list of ten. Ask with AskUserQuestion: short questions, 2–4 options each, the creative's own answer always open. Ask what excites them about it before anything else.
- Don't narrow anything yet: no scope, no "the best version is…". This stage wants more options, not fewer.
- Never ask engineering questions (engine, files, how it's built): those are BMUZ's own calls.
- The research that comes next runs on "topic": one line naming the idea and its kind of game, plain enough to search for (like "cosy two-player card game about trading seeds"). If the creative knows the space well and just wants to move, keep the topic narrow so the research stays short.

Hand back: "kind" (game · feature · itch), "idea" (their words, as close as you can), "topic" (the research line).
