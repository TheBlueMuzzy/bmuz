Purpose: fill the gaps that would cost the most later, in gather mode only — what they have, never which one. About 10 minutes: usually two rounds, three at most. The creative just saw "the game so far" (version 1); the recap above is its raw form.

Asking (every Ideate step):
- Every question goes through the question box (AskUserQuestion). Never ask anything in plain text: a question in plain chat isn't seen as a question and the run waits. Their own words always go in "Other".
- Nothing they must read goes in plain text in the same turn as the box: it ends up hidden behind it. Put it inside the question or the options' descriptions. At most one short line before the box.

A round = ONE AskUserQuestion call with 2 to 4 questions. Each question:
- says which gap it fills: the header is the need ("Frame", "Rules", "Skill", "Your ideas", "Smallest", "Feel", "Look"), and the question opens with it in a few words: "Frame — how is it played: phone, desktop, both? Which way up?"
- is gather mode: "What ideas do you have for…?", "What's already decided about…?", "Anything you remember about…?", "What's off the table for…?"
- offers quick answers as options, never a choice to make: "Not decided yet" (description: "leave it open") is always one of them; the others are common facts that save typing (platforms, player counts), with multiSelect when several can be true. Their own words in Other.

Never:
- "which one?", "pick one", or asking them to set a rule or pick a mechanism (that's Define)
- their own words read back as options ("of course all of these are selected. weak question")
- what the game so far already says (fixed or ideas) — ask only what's open
- fiction questions for a game with no fiction; jargon (MDA, "toy", "this feeling" with nothing it refers to); who the audience is; the dream; "what excites you"
- "keep going?" — you decide when the gaps that matter are asked

Order (costliest first, from the rework record):
1. Frame, whatever is open of it: how it's held and played (phone upright / sideways, desktop, both), players and opponents, online and what happens when someone leaves, goes quiet or joins late, how long one game lasts, controls.
2. Rules' shape and the real skill: how a game ends and how it scores; "What does a good player do that a new player doesn't?"
3. Their own half-formed ideas the material points at ("What do 2–10 do — anything you remember?", "the ideas you can't quite remember").
4. The smallest version worth playing: "What's the smallest version you'd want to play first?"
5. Feel: how we'll know it works ("What would you see a player do if it's working?"), only if open. Look and sound only if nothing is there.
Round 2 follows what round 1 opened (they said "online" → leaving and joining). Stop when 1–4 are asked or after three rounds.
