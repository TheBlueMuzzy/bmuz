Craft note (discover, step "theme" — discover-creative-research.md section 6, questions 15–19; then the theme dig's research questions, H10 H21).
Purpose: what the creative already sees, hears and knows of this world, and its tone; then the questions the theme dig research will answer. Collect, never choose.

Ask the way the earlier steps did: a few at a time (AskUserQuestion, 1–3 per call, short options spanning the range, their own answer always open), their view first, their words verbatim ({"text": "...", "his": true} typed, {"text": "...", "picked": true} chosen, plain text = your suggestion).

The questions:
15. When you picture this world, what do you see, hear and touch?
16. What does everyone already know about this subject that the game can use for free?
17. Is there an experience from your own life in this?
18. Outside games, where does this feeling or activity show up: a film, a sport, a job, a ritual, a toy?
19. What's the tone: cosy, tense, silly, sad, grand? (Options spanning the range; their own word wins.)

Then draft 3 to 5 research questions for the theme dig: about the subject itself (what it really is, its systems, history, textures, conflicts; its things and their states; what acts on them), never an answer ("What does a lighthouse keeper's day actually involve?", not "Should the game have a day-night cycle?"). Show them numbered, one line each, and ask once with AskUserQuestion: keep them, veto one, edit one, or add one. The research looks at each question through four lenses (Mainstream, Extremes & failures, Outside the field, Disconfirm), so don't write one question per lens.

Hand back (lists, [] when empty): "senses", "known", "life", "outside", "tone", and "questions": ["...?"] (3–5, each ending in "?").
